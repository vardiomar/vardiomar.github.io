### 📄 C.I.A Triad
<a id="writeup-1"></a>
 
**Course / Context:** CYSE/IT 200T - Cybersecurity, Technology & Society
 
**Date:** Fall 2026
 
🔗 [Read the full write-up](/CIA_Triad_write-up.pdf)
 
**Summary:** This paper examines the CIA triad. It explains how authentication and authorization help protect information and systems. It uses the 2015 Anthem data breach to show how phishing and weak access controls can expose sensitive information.
 
**Key Takeaways:**
- Confidentiality protects information from unauthorized access.
- Integrity ensures data remains accurate and trustworthy.
- Availability ensures authorized users can access systems when needed.
- Authentication verifies who a user is, while authorization determines what they can access.
- MFA, access controls, employee training, and monitoring help reduce security risks.

### 📄 Human Factors in Cybersecurity
<a id="writeup-2"></a>
 
**Course / Context:** CYSE/IT 200T - Cybersecurity, Technology & Society
 
**Date:** Fall 2026
 
🔗 [Read the full write-up](/Human_Factor_in_Cybersecurity.pdf)
 
**Summary:** This paper explores how human behavior can create cybersecurity risks even when strong technical defenses are in place. It focuses on threats such as phishing, social engineering, weak passwords, and poor security practices, while emphasizing the importance of security awareness and organizational culture.
 
**Key Takeaways:**
- Human behavior is a significant part of the cybersecurity attack surface.
- Phishing and social engineering exploit human decision-making.
- Weak and reused passwords can increase the impact of credential compromise.
- Security policies should encourage employees to report mistakes and suspicious activity.
- MFA, least privilege, access controls, training, and monitoring can reduce human-related risks.

### 📄 SCADA Systems and ICS
<a id="writeup-3"></a>
 
**Course / Context:** CYSE/IT 200T - Cybersecurity, Technology & Society
 
**Date:** Fall 2026
 
🔗 [Read the full write-up](/SCADA_Systems_and_ICS.pdf)
 
**Summary:** This paper examines SCADA systems and their role in monitoring and coordinating critical infrastructure and industrial processes. It highlights how securing OT/ICS environments differs from traditional IT because cyber incidents can affect physical operations, public services, reliability, and safety
 
**Key Takeaways:**
- SCADA environments commonly include PLCs, RTUs, HMIs, and supervisory systems.
- OT security must account for safety, reliability, and operational requirements.
- Increased network connectivity can create additional attack paths.
- Patching industrial systems can be difficult because changes may affect operations.
- Network segmentation, strong access controls, monitoring, incident response, and recovery planning improve SCADA security.

## 🧠 Cybersecurity and Social Science
<a id="cyse-social-science"></a>
 
**Course / Context:** CYSE/IT 200T - Cybersecurity, Technology & Society
 
**Date:** Fall 2026
 
**Overview:** Understanding the Human Side of Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is about more than computers, networks, and technology. It also involves understanding people, their behaviors, and the decisions they make online. Through CYSE 201S, I have learned how psychology, sociology, and criminology help explain why cybercrimes happen and how people become victims.

As a cybersecurity student, I believe protecting people is just as important as protecting systems. Understanding how cybercriminals manipulate trust, use fear, and exploit human mistakes can help us develop better ways to prevent cyberattacks.

**Three Important Connections**
 
### 🖼️ Image 1: Social Engineering and Human Psychology
 
![A person receiving a suspicious email or phishing message.](/phishing.jpeg)
 
**Why this shows cybersecurity and social science:** Social engineering shows how attackers manipulate people into sharing sensitive information or giving unauthorized access. Learning to recognize these tactics helps us understand why cybersecurity awareness is important.

### 🖼️ Image 2: Cybercrime and Its Impact on Victims
 
![A person looking concerned while reviewing a compromised computer or suspicious bank transaction.](/hacked.png)
 
**Why this shows cybersecurity and social science:** Cybercrime can cause financial losses, emotional stress, and privacy loss. Studying the effects on victims reminds us that cybersecurity is ultimately about protecting people, not just devices.
 
---
 
### 🖼️ Image 3: Teamwork and Cybersecurity Awareness
 
![A team working together to investigate a cyber threat or improve security.](/teamwork.jpg)

### 📊 PowerPoint Presentation
<a id="presentation"></a>
 
#### 📄 Human Engineering: Hacking The Mind
 
**Course / Context:** CYSE/IT 200T - Cybersecurity, Technology & Society
 
**Date:** Fall 2026
 
🔗 [View the full presentation](/CYSE201S_Social%20Engineering_%20Hacking%20the%20Mind.pdf)
 
**Summary:** Social engineering is a cyberattack technique that manipulates people into sharing sensitive information or giving unauthorized access. My presentation, Social Engineering: Hacking the Mind, explains how attackers use human psychology, including trust, fear, urgency, and curiosity, to trick their victims. It also covers real-world examples, the steps attackers follow, and ways to reduce risks. The main message is that cybersecurity is not just about protecting technology; it is also about understanding and protecting people.
 
**Key Takeaways:**
- Social engineering targets people. Attackers manipulate human behavior instead of relying only on technical weaknesses.
- Psychology plays a major role. Attackers exploit trust, fear, urgency, authority, curiosity, and greed.
- There are different attack methods. Common social engineering tactics include phishing, impersonation, pretexting, and phone or text scams.
- Awareness helps prevent attacks. Verifying unusual requests, reporting suspicious activity, and slowing down before clicking links can reduce risks.
- People and technology must work together. Security awareness training, multifactor authentication (MFA), email filtering, and security monitoring help protect individuals and organizations.
